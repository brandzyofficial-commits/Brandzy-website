How to invoice brands as an influencer in India

Getting a brand deal is half the job. Getting paid on time is the other half. Here is what to put on your invoice, where GST and TDS come in, and how to follow up without awkward chats.

Updated 3 October 2026 · Written by the Brandzy team. This is general information, not tax advice; check your own situation with a chartered accountant.

Invoice checklist
  • Invoice number (unique and in sequence) and invoice date
  • Your name or business name, address, PAN, and GSTIN if you are registered
  • The brand's legal name, billing address and GSTIN if they have one
  • Campaign name and each deliverable, for example "1 Instagram reel + 3 stories"
  • Amount, with GST shown as a separate line if you charge it
  • Due date and payment terms, for example "due within 15 days"
  • Your bank account or UPI details

Before you invoice: agree the terms in writing

Most payment trouble starts before the invoice. In the email or contract that confirms the deal, write down the fee, the deliverables, the posting dates, usage rights, and when you will be paid. "Payment within 30 days of the invoice" is far easier to chase than "after the campaign".

For bigger deals, ask for part of the fee up front, such as 50% on signing and 50% after the content goes live.

GST basics for creators

  • When you need it: GST registration is generally required once your turnover from services crosses ₹20 lakh in a financial year (₹10 lakh in special category states). Some brands and agencies ask for a GSTIN even below that.
  • On the invoice: if you are registered, show your GSTIN, the brand's GSTIN, and the GST amount as a separate line from your fee.
  • Brands abroad: work for foreign brands paid in foreign currency can be treated as an export of services, which has its own GST rules.

Ask a chartered accountant which rate and code apply to your work.

TDS: why the brand paid you less

Brands in India often deduct TDS (tax deducted at source) before paying you, so the amount in your bank is lower than your invoice. That money is not lost: it is deposited against your PAN, shows up in your Form 26AS or Annual Information Statement, and counts toward your tax when you file your return. Ask the brand for the TDS certificate.

Free products you receive for promotion can also count as income, and brands may deduct TDS on them above a yearly threshold.

Following up on late payments

  1. On the due date: a short, friendly reminder with the invoice attached again.
  2. One week late: a second reminder, copying the person who approved the campaign.
  3. Two to three weeks late: ask for a firm payment date, and pause new work for that brand until it is paid.

Keep every reminder in writing, and keep a record of when you sent each one.

Frequently asked questions

Can I invoice a brand without GST registration?

Yes, if you are below the registration threshold and not otherwise required to register. Do not charge GST if you are not registered.

How long do brands take to pay?

Terms of 15 to 45 days after the invoice are common, and agency campaigns often take longer. Agree the terms before you start.

Should I invoice the brand or the agency?

Invoice whoever signed the agreement with you and is paying you. If an agency booked you, the invoice usually goes to the agency.
